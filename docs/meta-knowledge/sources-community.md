# Meta Certified Community Manager (600-101) — official source notes

Researched 2026-10-04. Every item below was read on an official Meta page on that date (URL given). Nothing comes from exam dumps or practice-test questions. Numeric specs can change; re-check the cited page before relying on them.

## Exam

- **Name (as shown on the official page today):** "600-101: Meta Certified Community Management" (formerly marketed as "Meta Certified Community Manager"; the Credly badge URL still uses `meta-certified-community-manager`, the badge title reads "Meta Certified Community Management").
- **Exam code:** 600-101
- **Official exam page:** https://www.facebook.com/business/learn/certification/exams/600-101-exam
- **Certification catalogue page (lists it as a skill-based, beginner-level credential):** https://www.facebook.com/business/learn/certification
- **Official description:** identifies candidates who can "build, scale and sustain an online community using community-building strategies and tools."
- **Audience:** community leaders, brand managers and social managers who manage, scale and connect online communities and understand policies and regulations.
- **Format (from the official exam page):** 60 questions, 105 minutes, passing score 700 out of 1,000.
- **Languages:** English, French, German, Indonesian, Portuguese (Brazilian), Spanish (LATAM). No Traditional Chinese.
- **Validity:** 24 months from the day earned.
- **Recertification:** (1) "refresh" — self-paced online courses (about two hours) plus an unproctored pass/fail assessment, only before expiry, retake after 24 hours, unlimited; or (2) retake the full exam (five-day wait after a failed attempt).
- **Badge / earning criteria (Credly, issuer Meta):** pass 600-101, acknowledge adherence to Meta's Community Standards, renew within 24 months. Listed skills: Community Building, Community Management, Community Strategy, Content Management, Content Strategy, Data Reporting, Facebook, Instagram, Social Media, WhatsApp. https://www.credly.com/org/facebook-blueprint/badge/meta-certified-community-manager
- **Official learning resources linked from the exam page:**
  - Study guide landing page: https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/209728 (links to a PDF titled "community-management-study-guide _ may-2026" on Meta's asset host; not opened, see Gaps)

## Outline

Source for the whole section: https://www.facebook.com/business/learn/certification/exams/600-101-exam (English wording read via `?locale=en_US`). Percentages are the published weights.

1. **Establish community parameters (7%)**
   - Develop community goals, objectives, mission, purpose, values and KPIs
   - Create and assess community guiding principles
2. **Develop community strategies and processes (31%)**
   - Cultivate and evolve a platform strategy
   - Create an effective audience strategy
   - Analyze a community branding strategy
   - Determine appropriate operational workflows
   - Define a team structure and management strategy
   - Determine a launch strategy
   - Identify and leverage strategic partnerships
3. **Make strategic content decisions (9%)**
   - Create and curate relevant content using a variety of tactics for specific target audiences within a community
   - Implement goal-driven activities
4. **Engage and moderate the community (25%)**
   - Grow and sustain a community
   - Address and resolve crises and conflicts
   - Implement engagement tactics based on audience segmentation
   - Adhere to community standards and terms of service
5. **Measure and analyze community success (16%)**
   - Analyze content and engagement performance
   - Examine data to conduct quantitative and qualitative reporting
   - Establish a feedback collection, learning and implementation process
6. **Monetization (10%)** — the English page repeats the heading "Measure and analyze community success" for this section (apparent page typo); the Traditional Chinese version of the same page titles it as measuring and analyzing monetization.
   - Establish opportunities for revenue generation
   - Apply appropriate monetization strategies

Note: the published weights add up to 98%, not 100% (7+31+9+25+16+10), exactly as shown on the official page.

### Organic content and distribution

- **F1.** Facebook ranks content in four steps: inventory (what was posted), signals (who posted, when, etc.), predictions (how likely the person is to comment, spend time, watch to completion, find it informative) and a relevance score; posts are ordered by that score. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/718033381901819 — D3 / D5
- **F2.** The predictions that feed the score explicitly include likelihood that a post is clickbait and likelihood that it links to a low-quality webpage, alongside likelihood to click, spend time, like/comment/share and be found informative. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/718033381901819 — D3
- **F3.** Engagement bait = urging people to like, share, comment or take other actions to artificially boost engagement and reach; posts and Pages using it receive less distribution. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/259911614709806 — D3 / D4
- **F4.** Named engagement-bait types: react baiting (asking for a reaction), comment baiting (asking for a specific word, number, phrase or emoji), share baiting (asking to share with friends), vote baiting (asking to vote via reactions, comments or shares). Genuine requests for help, advice or recommendations are distinguished from bait. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/259911614709806 — D3 / D4
- **F5.** Clickbait = a headline or body text that pushes people to click while withholding crucial information or exaggerating; such posts get reduced distribution. Meta's own "don't" examples are a headline that hides who/what, and a headline that sensationalises a claim. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/503640323442584 — D3
- **F6.** Recommended alternative to clickbait: headlines and body text that set accurate expectations, explicit text prompts and calls to action instead of missing information, and adding your own voice to drive genuine conversation. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/503640323442584 — D3
- **F7.** Posting tips: post frequently with new, high-quality content (diversity rules stop people seeing too much from one publisher), be timely on topical stories, and ask whether people would share or recommend the post. Meta states there is no single right time, number or type of post that works for everyone. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/276989996258978 — D2 / D3
- **F8.** Meta advises trying its range of storytelling tools (Live, video, stories, photos, etc.) and watching what the audience responds to, because what is "informative" differs per person. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/246791142703681 — D3
- **F9.** Live best practice: announce ahead with a scheduled live and a written post, check connection strength, write a description that tells people what the broadcast is about, collect audience questions in comments beforehand, and allow time at the start for viewers to join. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/246791142703681 — D3 / D4
- **F10.** Fake engagement (coordinated artificial distribution, many Pages or fake accounts pushing the same content) and long, distracting captions with an excessive number of hashtags lead to reduced distribution and loss of monetization eligibility. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2867407553519694 — D4 / D6
- **F11.** Pages and profiles get the most distribution when they consist mainly of original content the owner filmed or created; repeatedly sharing unoriginal content loses monetization access and reach, and duplicate videos are demoted in favour of the original. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2867407553519694 — D3 / D6
- **F12.** Page growth checklist from Meta: pick accurate categories, add profile picture and cover photo, complete profile info, add an action button, turn on Recommendations, keep address and hours updated, post when the audience is online, and use Insights to refine targeting. Since August 2025 people can only follow Pages, not like them (they can still like posts). — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/464618030623795 — D2 / D5

### Community standards, spam and moderation

- **F13.** Meta's Community Standards rest on voice, authenticity, safety, privacy and dignity; they apply to everyone worldwide and to all content types including AI-generated content, across Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and Threads. — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/ — D1 / D4
- **F14.** Spam policy prohibits buying, selling or exchanging engagement (likes, shares, views, follows, clicks), offering cash or monetary value in exchange for engagement, and requiring people to engage (like, share) to gain access to exclusive content. — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/ — D4
- **F15.** Spam policy also prohibits deceptive links (cloaking, misleading redirects, look-alike domains) and imitating platform features (for example a fake poll or fundraiser) to drive clicks, plus posting at very high frequency. — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/ — D4
- **F16.** Hateful conduct = attacks on people based on protected characteristics: race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, caste, sexual orientation, gender identity, sex, disability, serious disease. The policy targets attacks on people, not criticism of ideas or institutions. — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/hate-speech/ — D4
- **F17.** Page comment-moderation tools: hide comments containing chosen words, phrases or emojis; profanity filter; comment ranking (most relevant first); country and age restrictions; review of tags and tagged posts before they appear; blocking users, apps and Pages. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/845417592621623 — D4
- **F18.** On ads, anyone who can see the ad on Facebook, Instagram or Threads can generally react or comment; admins can like and reply (Facebook), hide or report comments, and pre-empt problems with the profanity filter (Facebook) or Hidden Words (Instagram, Threads). — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1129470964230971 — D4
- **F19.** Live chat moderation ladder: suspend a viewer (cannot comment for 15 minutes), ban from that stream, ban from all live streams, ban from the Page. Only available to Pages and profiles in professional mode; on new Pages the people moderating need community manager access. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1394099917683750 — D4 / D2
- **F20.** Group admins can ask up to 3 membership/participation questions (200 characters each; checkboxes, multiple choice or written answer). Answering is not mandatory, but Admin Assist can auto-approve or decline based on whether questions were answered. — https://www.facebook.com/help/200755420421098 — D2 / D4
- **F21.** Private groups can use "limited membership": people join without approval, can see and react, but cannot post, comment or chat until an admin approves them. — https://www.facebook.com/help/200755420421098 — D2 / D4

### Promotions, Pages terms and branded content

- **F22.** A Facebook promotion (contest, sweepstakes) must include a complete release of Facebook by each entrant and an acknowledgement that it is not sponsored, endorsed or administered by, or associated with, Facebook. — https://www.facebook.com/policies/pages_groups_events/ — D4
- **F23.** Facebook promotions may run on Pages, Groups, Events or in apps, but personal timelines and friend connections must not be used to administer them: no "share on your timeline to enter", "share on a friend's timeline for more entries" or "tag your friends to enter". — https://www.facebook.com/policies/pages_groups_events/ — D4
- **F24.** Instagram promotions: the organiser is responsible for lawful operation (official rules, eligibility such as age and residency, regulatory approvals), must include a complete release of Instagram and the not-sponsored/endorsed/administered acknowledgement, and must not inaccurately tag content or encourage people to do so. — https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/179379842258600 — D4
- **F25.** Page names must accurately reflect the Page and may not be only generic terms, use improper capitalisation or excessive symbols, or mislead about official status; cover photos must not be deceptive, misleading or infringe copyright. If a Page collects user data directly it must make clear that the Page, not Facebook, is collecting it and obtain consent. — https://www.facebook.com/policies/pages_groups_events/ — D1 / D2
- **F26.** Branded content = a creator's or publisher's content that features or is influenced by a business partner for an exchange of value (payment or free gifts). It must be posted with the branded content tool, tagging the partner with their prior permission, and must carry any disclosures the law requires. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/221149188908254 — D6 / D2
- **F27.** Branded content may not promote, among others: tobacco and vaping, drugs, unsafe supplements, weapons, adult products (except family planning and contraception), payday loans, multilevel marketing, initial coin offerings, state lotteries. Alcohol, subscription services, financial products, cosmetic procedures and weight loss are allowed only with restrictions; dating, gambling, pharmacies, crypto and similar partners need written pre-authorization. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/221149188908254 — D6
- **F28.** Branded content format limits: no pre-, mid- or post-roll ads, no banner ads in video or images, no title card within the first three seconds, and interstitial cards later in the video no longer than three consecutive seconds. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/221149188908254 — D6 / D3
- **F29.** To earn money on Facebook, Pages, professional-mode profiles, events and groups must pass and stay compliant with the Partner Monetization Policies and the Content Monetization Policies. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/169845596919485 — D6

### Ads: objectives, review and policy

- **F30.** Ads Manager has six objectives: awareness, traffic, engagement, leads, app promotion, sales. Meta's instruction is to settle the business goal first and then pick the objective, and to expect the objective to change as the business grows. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1438417719786914 — D2
- **F31.** A boosted post is an ad made from an existing post, Story or Reel; it is billed as an ad but offers fewer objectives, placements, formats and targeting options than an ad built in Ads Manager. When boosting, the existing text can be kept or several text variations supplied for Meta to rotate. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/317083072148603 — D2
- **F32.** Ad review runs automatically before an ad goes live, is typically finished within 24 hours, covers image, video, text, targeting and the landing page, and ads can be re-reviewed and rejected after they start. Rejected ads can be edited or sent for another review; low-quality ads that break no rule may still perform worse. — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/ — D4
- **F33.** Personal attributes: ads must not assert or imply that the advertiser knows a person's race, ethnicity, religion, beliefs, age, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health condition, vulnerable financial status, voting status, trade union membership, criminal record or name. Copy should focus on the product's benefits. Meta's contrast pairs: "Depression counseling" is acceptable, "Depression getting you down?" is not; "Meet seniors" versus "Meet other seniors". — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/ — D4
- **F34.** Health and wellness ads: no statements that generate negative self-perception about appearance, no close-ups pinching fat, no claims to cure incurable diseases, no sensational exaggerated claims; ads for diet, weight loss and similar products must be targeted to people 18 or older. — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness — D4
- **F35.** Prohibited commercial practices in ads include deceptive pricing, unauthorised or fake endorsements, and guaranteed investment returns. — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/deceptive-content/prohibited-commercial-practices/ — D4
- **F36.** Ads must not contain profanity, including obscured, misspelled, symbol-substituted or emoji versions and profane gestures. — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/profanity/ — D4
- **F37.** Ads must not discriminate or encourage discrimination on personal attributes (race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, age, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, family status, disability, medical or genetic condition); housing, employment and financial-product ads must declare a Special Ad Category and use restricted targeting. — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/unacceptable-content/discriminatory-practices/ — D4

### Ad creative and specs (numeric values can change)

- **F38.** Recommended aspect ratios: 4:5 for single-image ads in Facebook Feed and 1:1 for Instagram Feed (both are supported in Feed); 9:16 for Stories, Status and Reels; 1:1 or 4:5, kept consistent, for carousel; 16:9 for in-stream video. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/103816146375741 — D3
- **F39.** Text overlay guidance: clean font, large enough size, contrasting colour; do not cover the visual; do not pack in several messages because an ad usually has one call to action; stay inside the safe zone. With disclaimers on Reels ads, leave the bottom 40% clear. — https://www.facebook.com/business/help/980593475366490 — D3
- **F40.** Facebook Feed image ad (traffic objective) as currently listed: JPG or PNG, 4:5, 1440 x 1800 px, primary text 50 to 150 characters, headline 27 characters, 30 MB maximum. — https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/image/facebook-feed/traffic — D3
- **F41.** Instagram Reels video ad as currently listed: MP4 or MOV, 9:16, 1440 x 2560 px, primary text 44 characters, up to 15 minutes, 4 GB maximum; keep roughly 14% at the top, 35% at the bottom and 6% at each side free of text and logos. — https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels — D3
- **F42.** Reels play with sound on by default; Meta reports that campaigns with 9:16 video with audio and key messages in the safe zone saw 2x delivery to Reels and 34.5% lower cost per result than image ads (Meta's own figures). — https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads — D3 / D5
